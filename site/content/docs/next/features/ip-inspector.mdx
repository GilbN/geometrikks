The IP inspector is a side sheet, or a docked panel on wide screens, about one address: how much it sent, how the server answered, what it asked for, where it came from, and what CrowdSec, Spamhaus and AbuseIPDB say about it. It covers the selected time range, and the range picker next to the address changes it without closing the inspector.

## Opening it

- From a table, open the IP actions menu after the address and choose Inspect IP. Access Logs, Geo Logs, Debug Logs, Analytics and the Security tables all have the menu.
- On the map, use the inspect button next to an IP in a location's details, in a banned IP's details, or in the Top IPs list of the map controls.
- From the inspector itself, the Analytics and Access logs buttons at the bottom open those pages filtered to the address. On a screen wide enough to show the page beside the sheet, the inspector stays open there.

## What it shows

The header names the address's city and country, its AS number and organization with a link to bgp.he.net, and, when CrowdSec is set up, a link to the address on CrowdSec CTI. A link to the address's page on AbuseIPDB is always there, and works without an AbuseIPDB key. Under it, a badge shows the active CrowdSec decision with its scenario and time left, and a DROP badge shows the Spamhaus listing with a link to its SBL record. With an AbuseIPDB key, a badge shows a public address's AbuseIPDB score. With the AbuseIPDB blacklist on, a mark shows an address on the blacklist. The score lookup spends one of your daily AbuseIPDB checks, and the free plan gets 1,000 a day. Opening the same IP again within 15 minutes spends none, unless the server restarted or checked 512 other IPs in between. [AbuseIPDB](./abuseipdb.mdx#the-score-in-the-ip-inspector) covers both. With CrowdSec write access, the ban or unban button sits next to the address.

Below that come:

- **Signals**, the chips described in the next section.
- **Stats**: requests, bytes sent, average response time, the split by status class, and a bar chart of requests over the range. Click a bar to zoom the time range to that hour or day; Back to returns to the range you had. For a banned address the chart marks the bars that came after the ban.
- **Targets**, the hosts the address hit, when the log records a host.
- **Paths** and **User agents**, its busiest requests and clients.
- **Also seen from**, every other location the address resolved to, each with a Fly to button that opens the map on that location.
- **Latest requests**, its 10 newest requests in the range with time, host, method, path, status and size.

The footer has Analytics, Access logs, Debug logs when the address sent malformed requests, and the button for an [abuse report](./abuse-reports.mdx).

## Signals

Each chip flags a pattern worth a second look. Hover a chip for its explanation. None of them bans anything.

| Chip | When it appears | Color |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `N% 4xx` | 50% or more of the address's requests got a 4xx response, counted only from 20 requests up | Amber, red from 70% |
| DROP ASN | The address belongs to an AS on Spamhaus's ASN-DROP list | Red |
| Hosting ASN | The address belongs to a network on the bundled hosting, VPN and datacenter list | Amber |
| `Still seen Nh after ban` | Requests kept arriving after the CrowdSec ban started. The chip adds how many requests came after it | Red |
| `N paths` | The address asked for 50 or more distinct paths in the range | Amber |
| `N malformed` | The parser flagged one or more of its lines as malformed | Gray |
| Burst | At least half of its requests landed in one hour or day bucket, counted only from 100 requests up | Amber |

Still seen after ban means one of two things. Either no bouncer enforces the decision, or the traffic reaches your proxy on a path the bouncer doesn't cover. The count is per bucket, so a ban in the middle of an hour can undercount by up to one bucket.

## The IP actions menu

Every IP in a table has a copy button and an IP actions button after it. The menu offers:

- Copy IP and Inspect IP.
- Fly to on the map, which opens the map on the address's location.
- Filter by IP, host, server, country or ASN, taken from the row, and Exclude for the values the page can exclude. A value already in a filter gets Stop filtering or Stop excluding instead. Which values appear depends on the page's filters.
- With CrowdSec write access, Ban IP with a choice of 1 hour, 4 hours, 24 hours, 7 days or Forever, or Unban IP for a banned address. A ban or unban waits 5 seconds before it reaches CrowdSec, behind a toast with Undo.
- For an address on a DROP list, a ban or unban of the whole DROP network.
- With AbuseIPDB reporting on, Report to AbuseIPDB…, which opens a report prefilled from what GeoMetrikks saw. The item says when the address was last reported, and stays disabled for 15 minutes after a report and on an address that isn't public. See [Report an IP](./abuseipdb.mdx#report-an-ip).

The menu's header shows the address with its CrowdSec decision, DROP listing and AbuseIPDB blacklist mark. In the footer of a record sheet, such as an access log row's details, the menu leaves out Copy, Inspect and Fly to, since the sheet has its own buttons for them.

## Docked beside the page

From 1600 px wide, the inspector docks beside the page by default, on every page it opens from. The page stays usable next to it, so you can scroll or filter a table while the inspector stays open. On the map, the map gets narrower to make room. Record sheets on Access Logs, Geo Logs, Debug Logs and the Security page's Alerts tab share that dock by default, so it holds either the inspector or a record, never both at once. Opening a record while the inspector is docked puts the record in the dock, and Back returns to the inspector. Inspect IP in a docked record shows that address in the dock, and the inspector's header adds a link back naming the record, such as "Request #35395" or "Alert #14", with "Back returns here" beside it. That link appears only when the inspector was opened from a record. Back from the inspector returns to the record, and closing the inspector on a phone does the same. Alert sheets opened from the Active decisions tab or the map's Banned IPs popup still open over the page.

When you open an IP from a row on Access Logs History, Geo Logs or Debug Logs, the docked inspector marks that row with a tint and an accent bar on its left edge. The arrows in the dock header, or [`J` and `K`](./keyboard-shortcuts.mdx), step to the next or previous row's IP and skip rows with the IP already shown. A step past the last row of a page loads the next page and lands on its first row with another IP. A step back from the first row does the same with the previous page, from the bottom. The step skips up to five pages that hold only the IP shown. When it finds no other IP, it stops on the last row it reached, and the next step goes on from there. Each step replaces the inspector's history entry, so one press of Back still closes the dock. The mark and the arrows show only while the inspector is docked, and they go away when a page change, a filter or an auto refresh pushes the row out of the table.

The Dock / Undock button next to Close sits in both the inspector and record headers, moving them between the dock and the side sheet that dims the page. Undocking a record undocks the inspector too, and the other way round. Your browser remembers the choice. Below 1600 px, both open as the side sheet without that button. Close, or Escape while focus is in the dock, closes it.

Opening the inspector adds one history entry, whether from the page or from a docked record. Inspecting another address while docked replaces that entry, and docking or undocking adds none, so one press of Back closes the dock, or returns to the record if you opened the inspector from one. Clicking another row while a record is docked replaces the record's entry in the same way. The Analytics, Access logs and Debug logs buttons at the bottom open a new page as usual, so Back goes to the page you left first.

At 1600 px with the sidebar expanded, the Access Logs table scrolls sideways for its last columns. Collapse the sidebar or hide fields in the View menu to make room.

## On a phone

The inspector opens full screen. The IP actions menu opens as a drawer from the bottom, or from the right on a phone held sideways, and Ban opens a second drawer with the durations, the same one the map panels use.
